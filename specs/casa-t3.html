What this drawing is, and is not

A brief you can argue with — room sizes, adjacencies and circulation, at scale. It is not a licensable project. In Portugal the projeto de arquitetura submitted to the câmara has to be authored by a registered architect, so this exists to arrive at that meeting knowing what you want, not to replace it. Structure, spans, foundations, thermal and acoustic regulation, accessibility and drainage are all unresolved here.

Does €250k build it?

Portuguese turnkey construction runs roughly €1 200–1 800/m². Terrace and garage count at a reduced rate — they are cheaper per m² than heated space.

What changed, and what is still weak

  • Fixed: the suite was not a suite. Its closet and WC sat across a wall with no door from the bedroom — they would have been entered from the corridor. They now open off the bedroom.
  • Fixed: the guest WC was 14 m from the front door, at the far end past the kitchen. It now sits directly off the hall.
  • Fixed: a room with no way in. The old storage room was reachable only through the family bathroom. It is gone; the bathroom took the space and storage moved to the service side by the kitchen.
  • Still weak — the corridor. 17,1 m², a tenth of the house, doing nothing but connecting. Two floors, or bedrooms on both sides of a shorter corridor, would recover most of it.
  • Still weak — the office is shallow. 4,60 × 2,60 m works for a desk and shelving, not for two people or a meeting.
  • The suite opens off the entrance hall. Parents are far from the boys and a visitor sees the bedroom door. Some families want exactly that separation; if not, the distribuidor would need to extend west one bay.
  • The WC social has no window — it needs mechanical extraction, normal but a real cost line.
  • No structure yet. The 7,4 m sala span and the south glazing need a beam nobody has sized.
